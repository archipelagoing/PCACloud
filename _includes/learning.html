FROM NUMBERS TO NEBULOUS

Your data has a shape.

Principal component analysis (PCA) finds the directions in which your data varies most. We center your numeric columns, optionally scale them to equal variance, and project each row onto the first three principal components.

Each row becomes a soft particle in the cloud. Switch to Data points to see the exact projected positions. Density and particle size change the rendering, not the PCA results.

The explained variance tells you how much of the original variation these three dimensions preserve. Non-numeric columns are excluded; rows with missing numeric values are skipped. Large datasets are evenly sampled to 2,500 rows before PCA.

The sample is synthetic atmospheric data, created to demonstrate correlated measurements. The cloud is an artistic rendering, not a weather simulation.

The math behind the plot

EVRⱼ = λⱼ / Σ λℓ

λⱼ is the variance along component j, ordered from largest to smallest. Retained variance is the sum of the retained components’ ratios.

Z = X̃Vₖ   ·   X̂ = ZVₖᵀ
‖X̃ − X̂‖² / ‖X̃‖² = 1 − Σⱼ₌₁ᵏ EVRⱼ

X̃ is centered data, optionally standardized. Vₖ contains k orthonormal component directions. PCA maximizes retained variance and minimizes squared reconstruction error with the same projection. Error is measured in the preprocessed space.

Which measurements shape the cloud?

Loadings are the weights used to combine your features. The three largest absolute weights are highlighted; positive and negative weights point in opposite directions.

zᵢⱼ = Σℓ x̃ᵢℓ vℓⱼ

A point’s component coordinate is a weighted sum of its centered, optionally standardized measurements. Flipping all weights and scores for a component mirrors the axis without changing the PCA solution. These weights describe the projection, not causal importance.

PCA follows variance, so changing units can change its answer. Standardizing removes that dependence on units. It is a modeling choice: shared scales and meaningful low variance may justify leaving it off.

Why the covariance convention matters

This lesson uses population covariance and population standard deviations, dividing by n = 4, to match the assignment. The main cloud uses sample covariance and sample standard deviations, dividing by n − 1. With consistent preprocessing, component directions and explained-variance ratios agree; eigenvalues and standardized scores depend on the convention.

For numerical stability, unstandardized cloud inputs are also divided by one shared scale. This preserves directions and variance ratios. Display coordinates are uniformly resized to fit the scene; the lesson reports its own calculation units.

Variance measures information retained in this representation. It does not guarantee that groups or classes remain distinguishable. Soft particles and cloud density are visual effects, not statistical density estimates.
